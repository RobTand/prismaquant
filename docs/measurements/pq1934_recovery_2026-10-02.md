## Claims and limits

Timing uses local monotonic clocks and explicit CUDA completion fences.
Per-arm Torch Chrome traces, local power samples, exact raw Netdata API
responses and the existing `CaptureObserver` both-host stream are retained.
Known cross-host clock offset and variable clock-probe bounds leave clock
alignment and energy on **HOLD**. Successful raw collection does not establish
healthy alignment, GPU saturation, or useful work per joule. No production
telemetry gate was relaxed and no clock was stepped during the action.

The measurement tests routed-output equality for both down-capture modes and
both unlimited/16-row policies. It also injects one changed live BF16 bit and
requires the baseline and candidate to emit the same first mismatch refusal.
The exact staged-file authority and private pinned-copy ownership are unchanged.
Every exit synchronizes CUDA while the raw-buffer ExitStack still owns its
sources. Pinned staging is drained and the existing caching host allocator
released between projected-check timing and Hessian/prefix allocation, outside
both arms. Prospective guards include simultaneous file pagecache, sealed raw
buffers, live GPU weights, phase-specific pinned staging or hook Hessians and
prefixes; GPU demand remains a subset of the aggregate on shared-memory GB10.

This control establishes no full-campaign, export, serving, KL or bpp claim.
Production numerical behavior is required to remain equal. Only Astra may
accept or merge the recovery; no auto-merge, queue entry or live service change
was requested here.

## Actual result

The action finished on Sparklina with exit 0 in 709.12 seconds. Its terminal
receipt and source snapshot were independently inspected, and the local result
claim payload passed all hash checks. All twelve steady arms lasted at least
30 seconds; timings below are the mean of the two arm means, with explicit
CUDA completion per call.

| Resident L20 operation | Baseline ms/call | Candidate ms/call | Latency reduction | Baseline/candidate sampled W |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Per-expert derivation | 54.045 | 41.565 | 23.09% | 35.6 / 40.1 |
| Genuine production hook | 380.177 | 363.861 | 4.29% | 42.7 / 43.4 |
| 864 projected-unit checks | 5929.910 | 2113.300 | 64.36% | 13.2 / 15.6 |

Watts are arithmetic means of the local requested/observed `nvidia-smi`
samples, recorded beside each phase, compared only descriptively with the
approximately 140 W GB10 envelope. They are not energy integration or a
saturation finding. Every power recorder returned samples with zero errors.
The PB box window reports a 43.79 W peak against its separate 100 W declared
fallback floor; that floor is not the approximately 140 W device envelope.
Clock alignment and work-per-joule qualification remain on HOLD.

The hook clears the requested 2% local screen with a **4.29%** latency
reduction. Its ABBA means were 379.874, 363.818, 363.904 and 380.479 ms.
Warmup needed 512 real hook calls because the smallest nonzero routed expert
had one row. There were 648 active selected Linear units and 432 shared prefix
owners, all at 512 rows. The actual shared Hessian owners held 18,119,393,280
bytes; every timed hook arm preserved the prefix row counts. These are measured
closure counters, not a mirrored accumulator.

All four routed equality cases passed (72 zero-row experts), and the changed
BF16 live bit produced identical ordered baseline/candidate refusals. Host
sync instrumentation and Torch traces agree: derivation and hook stream syncs
changed from 288 to one, and projected-check syncs from 864 to one. In the first
derivation profile, `cudaStreamSynchronize` self CPU time was 27.49 ms before
and its count was one after. The first projected-check baseline spent 4.24 s
of self CPU time in `aten::equal`; the candidate's largest recorded self CPU
operation was `aten::copy_` at 1.04 s. The candidate still pays private source
staging work; this result does not establish a GPU-bound check or saturation.
The hook's profiles retain queue-full waits, GPU completion and the existing
Hessian work, as well as routing; its gain is consequently smaller than the
isolated derivation gain. Raw `self_device_seconds` is a sum of key-average
counters, which may overlap; it is not elapsed GPU wall time. The evidence
summary additionally records kernel-event duration sums with that limitation
explicit, and the Chrome traces remain the source for event inspection.

The final guard reports 69,070,045,184 conservative aggregate bytes and
35,651,584,000 CUDA-reserved bytes, below the respective 90/48 GiB envelopes.
